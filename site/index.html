What it is

Music from movement, no touch needed

Tarsier is firmware for the Adafruit Proximity Trinkey, a tiny USB board with an infrared proximity sensor. Plug it into a computer, tablet or phone, then move a hand (or an elbow, a foot, a head) above it to play notes, bend pitch or sweep a controller.

It shows up as a standard USB MIDI device called Tarsier, so it works without drivers in any DAW, synth or music app that accepts MIDI. It is an outcome of DMLab, the research and development arm of Drake Music, and began in 2015 as an extension of Diogo Moreda's Tone Ruler project. It follows the idea behind instruments like Soundbeam: any movement can make music, and every note sounds good.

Touch-free

Play by moving through an invisible beam above the sensor.

Always in tune

Notes are laid out on a scale, so nothing sounds wrong.

Expressive

Move faster to play louder; move closer to play higher.

Shows what it's doing

Two lights show the mode, your hand's position and every note.

What you need

A Trinkey, a USB port and something that makes sound

  • An Adafruit Proximity Trinkey.
  • A computer, tablet or phone with a USB-A port, or an adapter (USB-C to USB-A works fine).
  • Something that makes sound from MIDI: a DAW such as GarageBand, Ableton Live, Logic or Reaper, or a software synth. Free options include Helm, VCV Rack and Pure Data.
Install

About a minute, no software

You copy one file onto the board, much like copying a file onto a USB stick.

  1. Download

    Download Tarsier.uf2. Tagged versions are on the releases page.

  2. Plug it in

    Push the Trinkey into a USB port with the lights and sensor facing up.

  3. Double-tap reset

    Tap the small button near the USB plug twice, quickly. The lights turn green and a drive called TRINKEYBOOT appears.

  4. Copy the file

    Drag Tarsier.uf2 onto TRINKEYBOOT. The drive disappears by itself when the copy finishes. That means it worked.

  5. Play

    Tarsier restarts and the mode light slowly breathes green. Choose Tarsier as the MIDI input in your music software.

To put CircuitPython or other firmware back later, double-tap reset and copy that firmware's .uf2 onto TRINKEYBOOT in the same way.

Modes, pads and tips

  
The sensor between the two lights looks upwards. The beam starts about a hand's width above the board, and the closer you get, the higher the note.


  
Modes

  
Tap pad 1 to switch modes. The mode light changes colour.

  

    
ModeColourWhat happensGood for

    
      
1. BeamgreenThe beam is split into note zones on a scale. Each time you cross into a new zone, its note plays.Melodies, exploring sound

      
2. TriggerorangeOne note plays when your hand enters the beam and stops when it leaves.Drums, samples, cues

      
3. ThereminvioletA note plays on entry, then its pitch slides as you move.Sirens, swoops, leads

      
4. CCblueA controller (CC 1, the mod wheel) goes from 0 at the edge of the beam to 127 up close.Filters, effects, volume

Tips

  
    
    
  • Start with Beam mode and 5 zones. Fewer zones make each note easier to find.
    • 
    
  • Speed sets loudness. A quick sweep plays loud; a slow drift plays soft.
    • 
    
  • Theremin mode: set your synth's pitch-bend range to 12 semitones. With the usual 2, the whole beam covers only a tone.
    • 
    
  • Keep the space above clear. A low shelf or lampshade can look like a hand. Place the board where nothing hangs over it, then hold both pads to recalibrate.
    • 
    
  • Mount it where the player can reach. Tape it to a tray, armrest or table edge, so whatever part of the body moves most freely can pass over it.
    • 
  






  Lights
  
Reading the lights

  
Light 0 (by pad 1) shows the mode. Light 1 (by pad 2) shows the beam.

  

    
Looks likeYou seeIt means

    
      
Mode light slowly breathingReady and waiting. The colour shows the mode.

      
Beam light glowingA hand is in the beam. Brighter means closer. In Beam mode each note zone has its own colour.

      
Beam light flashes whiteA note was just played.

      
Both lights blink a few timesA setting changed. Count the blinks: that's the new mode, scale, octave or zone choice.

      
Mode light pulsing amberNot connected to a computer, or the computer is asleep.

      
Both lights blinking red quicklyThe sensor isn't responding. Unplug the board and plug it back in.

  Help
  
Troubleshooting

  

    TRINKEYBOOT doesn't appear
    
Double-tap the reset button a little faster or slower; the timing matters. Also try another USB port or adapter, because some cables and hubs only carry power.

  

  

    Tarsier doesn't appear in my music software
    
Restart the software after plugging Tarsier in; many apps only look for MIDI devices when they start. On Windows, if it still appears under its old name ("ProxLight Trinkey"), remove it in Device Manager and plug it in again.

  

  

    Notes play by themselves
    
Something above the sensor is being seen as a hand. Clear the space above the board, then hold both pads for a second to recalibrate.

  

  

    Nothing happens when I move
    
Use the tester above. If it shows messages, Tarsier is working: check that your music software is listening to MIDI channel 1 and has an instrument loaded.

  






  Make it yours
  
Customise and contribute

  
Every setting can be changed in config.h, including the MIDI channel, key, scales, beam length, sensitivity and brightness. The README explains how to build your own version.
